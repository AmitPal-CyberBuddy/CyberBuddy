Clickjacking: prove it in one scan

Clickjacking (UI redressing) loads a real page inside an attacker's frame and tricks a victim into clicking it. Two response headers decide whether that is possible — and only one of them still matters most.

Aligned with OWASP WSTG-CLNT-09 · CWE-1021 — UI redressing

Open the Clickjacking Validator

The attack in one paragraph

An attacker embeds your page in an <iframe> on a page they control, makes the frame transparent or covers it with their own decoy UI, and positions a bait button exactly over one of your real controls. The victim thinks they clicked “Play”; the browser delivers a genuine, authenticated click to “Transfer funds” or “Grant access” on your origin. Nothing is injected and no token is stolen — the victim's own session does the work, which is why clickjacking survives CSRF tokens and SameSite cookies.

The two controls

HeaderWhat it doesVerdict
X-Frame-Options DENY or SAMEORIGIN blocks framing in current browsers. ALLOW-FROM is obsolete and ignored. Legacy. Keep it, but do not rely on it alone.
CSP frame-ancestors Names exactly which origins may frame the page: 'none', 'self', an allowlist, or the wide-open *. The modern control. Browsers honor it over X-Frame-Options.

That override is the trap people miss: a page can send X-Frame-Options: DENY and still be framable if its CSP says frame-ancestors *. A CSP that exists but omits frame-ancestors does nothing for framing — no other directive substitutes for it — and a frame-ancestors that only appears on Content-Security-Policy-Report-Only is telemetry, not enforcement.

Confirm it with the validator

  1. Open the Clickjacking Validator and paste a URL you are authorized to test.
  2. Press Validate. The scan is a read-only GET; it reads the response headers and tries to frame the page.
  3. If the real site renders inside the frame, it is framable — that render is the evidence.
  4. Press Show PoC overlay to composite a decoy attacker page over the live target, which is the screenshot a triager wants to see.
  5. Screenshot or export the report card. It carries the target, final URL, HTTP status, timestamp, and which engine produced the result.

The risk it prints follows the published rules: permissive frame-ancestors or no effective control is High, X-Frame-Options alone is Medium, a restrictive frame-ancestors is Low. The full table is on the methodology page.

The fix

Send both headers on every HTML response, not just the login page:

Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors 'none'
X-Frame-Options: DENY

Use frame-ancestors 'self' if your own pages frame each other, or an explicit origin list if a partner must embed you — never *, and never a bare scheme. Frame-busting JavaScript is not a fix; sandboxed frames neutralise it. Then re-run the validator: a protected page refuses to render and the report card flips to Low.
